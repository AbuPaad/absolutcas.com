What it is

Exam-legal machines are slow by rule, not by physics.

An exam-approved calculator is deliberately held back, because the rules require it. NumOS sits in the gap: the ergonomics and muscle memory of a calculator, without the artificial ceiling. You keep the device you already know and swap the board underneath it.

NumOS is a fork of NeoCalculator that diverges freely. It is a real operating system on the device: a shell, an app set and a screen language of its own. Graphing, equations, matrices, notes, a Game Boy core, an AI arm, and a skin redrawn to look like the calculator it is standing in for. The CAS engine underneath is inherited rather than written here, which is exactly why the work goes into the board, the apps and the AI bridge. Much more is coming.

Shell
Stock Casio. The board and the screen come out, the shell stays.
Keypad
6 by 9 matrix on a TCA9555 over I2C. The legend follows the fx-82 closely on purpose.
Firmware
A fork of NeoCalculator that diverges freely. On-device OTA is the target.
Promise
No date is promised. The hardware is not finished and will not be rushed.

Why it exists

A graphing calculator costs a few hundred because the rules say what it may do, not because the silicon costs anything like that. Exam legality is a regulatory moat and the price sits behind it. The AI calculator mods doing the rounds are the other half of the same problem: exam aids with no life outside the exam hall.

What the calculator in your bag is missing is not power. It is permission, and permission is a decision made above the device - one that says a machine may not graph, may not store, may not talk to anything. That decision is what this board steps around. The press did the same thing to the same kind of rule: a machine held back by a regulation rather than by physics.

One board, the whole family

fx-82 and fx-991 variants share the board. Casio streamlined and regionalised the line, so a single board covers them.

About $25 a unit

Build cost at a 50 unit order. Kit target around $65, donor device not included.

CrowdSupply runs the campaign, the payment and the shipping.

Future intended features

None of this is promised on a date. It is the intended direction.

Camera

An OV2640 over SCCB on a magnetic pogo-pin header, so the module can only mate the right way. Sold as an add-on.

OTA

Firmware updates over Wi-Fi, no cable, done from the device.

Storage

microSD for ROMs, notes and settings.

Radio

Device-hosted hotspot for setup. No app, no account.
