What it is
Exam-legal machines are slow by rule, not by physics.

An exam-approved calculator is deliberately held back, because the rules require it. NumOS sits in the gap: the ergonomics and muscle memory of a calculator, without the artificial ceiling. You keep the device you already know and swap the board underneath it.

  • ShellStock Casio. The board and the screen come out, the shell stays.
  • Keypad6 by 9 matrix on a TCA9555 over I2C. The legend follows the fx-82 closely on purpose.
  • FirmwareA fork of NeoCalculator that diverges freely. On-device OTA is the target.
  • PromiseNo date is promised. The hardware is not finished and will not be rushed.

Where it goes
One board fits the whole family.

Casio streamlined and regionalised its line, so the fx-82 and fx-991 variants are close enough mechanically and electrically that a single board covers them. That is where the drop-in claim gets its leverage.

  • Familyfx-82 and fx-991 variants share the board.
  • InstalledRoughly 350 million devices in the serviceable market.
  • Build costAbout $25 per unit at a 50 unit order.
  • Kit targetAround $65, donor device not included.
  • ChannelCrowdSupply runs the campaign, the payment and the shipping.
